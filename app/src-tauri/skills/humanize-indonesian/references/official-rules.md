# What the official sources say (Badan Bahasa, EYD Edisi V, KBBI)

Use this file when you need to say "this is wrong" and be right. It was checked on 2026-11-23 against the sources named below. Each rule says how well it is backed.

- **Read** = the text of Badan Bahasa or KBBI was read.
- **Inference** = no official page says it word for word; it follows from a rule that does.
- **Not verified** = do not present it as official.

Sources (Badan Bahasa now sits under Kemendikdasmen; the old kemdikbud addresses may not open):

- EYD Edisi V (2022), one page per rule at `ejaan.kemendikdasmen.go.id`.
- Seri Penyuluhan Bahasa Indonesia (Badan Bahasa, 2019): *Bentuk dan Pilihan Kata* (Mustakim), *Kalimat* (Sasangka), *Paragraf*.
- KBBI VI Daring at `kbbi.kemendikdasmen.go.id`.
- The Tata Bahasa Baku Bahasa Indonesia (TBBBI) was **not read directly**. Anything that depends on it below is marked.

## Sentences

| Rule | What the source says | Backing |
|---|---|---|
| **"di mana" and "yang mana" are question words**, not connectors | *Bentuk dan Pilihan Kata* calls using them to join clauses "penggunaan kata yang tidak cermat". Fixes it gives: *di mana* → *tempat*; *yang mana* → *yang*; joining two clauses → *dan*. Real questions stay: "Rapat itu akan diselenggarakan di mana?" | Read |
| **Do not stack two connectors** (*meskipun … tetapi*, *walaupun … namun*, *karena … maka*, *jika … maka*) | *Kalimat*: the first word makes a subordinate clause, *tetapi* joins equal parts, *namun* links sentences; together they leave no main clause. Use one. Popular sites list *meskipun … tetapi* as acceptable; the official books do not. Not covered: a *maka* after a long subject or a *bahwa* clause | Read |
| **A preposition cannot mark the subject** (*Bagi pemenang akan mendapatkan…*, *Dalam penelitian ini menunjukkan…*) | Older Pusat Bahasa practical guide (*Buku Praktis*): *dalam*, *dengan*, *bagi* cannot mark the subject | Read (older guide) |
| **No preposition after a verb that has its own object** (*membahas tentang*, *membicarakan mengenai*) | *Kalimat*: a preposition makes a prepositional phrase, which cannot be the object | Read |
| **Economy of words (kehematan)** | *Bentuk dan Pilihan Kata*: if a shorter word or phrase says the same, use it. Its examples: *melakukan kunjungan* → *berkunjung*; *mengajukan saran* → *menyarankan*; *mengeluarkan pemberitahuan* → *memberitahukan*; *disebabkan oleh fakta* → *karena*. *Melakukan pengujian terhadap* → *menguji* follows the same principle but is not one of its examples | Read; the last one Inference |
| **Doubled words (mubazir)** | Pick one of: *agar supaya*, *adalah merupakan*, *demi untuk*, *seperti misalnya*, *contohnya seperti*, *lalu kemudian*, *kalau seandainya*, *sangat … sekali*, *hanya … saja*, *membahas tentang*, *maksud daripada*. Not *Para guru-guru*, *Sejumlah desa-desa*, *beberapa temuan-temuan* (one plural marker). *Daripada* is for comparing: *terbuat dari kayu*, not *terbuat daripada kayu*. No *dan sebagainya* after *misalnya* or *seperti* | Read |
| ***dalam rangka untuk*** | KBBI: *dalam rangka* means "dalam kaitan dengan" and its example has no *untuk*. No Badan Bahasa page condemns *dalam rangka untuk* by name | Inference |
| ***naik ke atas***, ***disebabkan karena***, ***para hadirin*** as official examples | Widely taught, but not found in the official texts that were read. The principle (no doubled meaning) holds | Not verified as official examples |
| **Passive voice** | No official preference for the active was found. *Kalimat* uses passives in its own corrected examples (*diberikan oleh pemerintah*). It corrects a passive only when the agent is lost or several *oleh* phrases pile up | Read; "Indonesian prefers the active" Not verified |
| **Connectives between sentences** | *Paragraf* lists *selain itu*, *dengan demikian*, *namun*, *walaupun demikian* and the like as normal, sorted by the relation they show. No official word on overuse or on *hal ini*. EYD V: a comma follows them ("Oleh karena itu, dia memperoleh…") | Read; overuse as a fault is a style judgement |
| **Starting a sentence with *tetapi* or *dan*** | *Kalimat*: *tetapi* is a coordinating word, *namun* links sentences. A Balai Bahasa page says *akan tetapi* and *namun* open a sentence and *tetapi* sits in the middle after a comma. EYD V has no page that bans it | Secondary; Inference. Normal in casual writing, usually corrected in formal writing |

## Spelling: the standard form (baku) of words that are often wrong

From the *Bentuk dan Pilihan Kata* table and KBBI VI entries (the other form is "tidak baku"):

aktivitas (not aktifitas) • analisis (analisa) • akta (akte) • biaya (beaya) • diagnosis (diagnosa) • efektif (effektif) • efisien (effisien) • Februari (Pebruari) • November (Nopember) • foto (photo) • fotokopi • hipotesis (hipotesa) • jadwal (jadual) • Jumat (Jum'at) • kualitas (kwalitas) • kuantitas (kwantitas) • kuesioner (questioner) • kuitansi (kwitansi) • metode (methode, metoda) • objek (obyek) • persen (prosen) • persentase (prosentase) • produktivitas (produktifitas) • Rabu (Rebo) • risiko (resiko) • sistem (sistim) • teknik (tehnik) • praktik (praktek) • izin (ijin) • nasihat (nasehat) • apotek (apotik) • hierarki (hirarki) • silakan (silahkan)

Widely taught as standard but not re-read in this check: andal (not handal), hakikat (hakekat), antre (antri).

Note: *analisa*, *resiko*, *praktek* and *silahkan* are common in everyday writing. In a formal text or an edit that must be correct, change them. In a quote, leave them.

## Punctuation

- **Tanda pisah (—)** is correct in three uses (EYD V): to frame an aside that is not the core of the sentence ("Keberhasilan itu—kita sependapat—dapat dicapai jika kita mau berusaha keras."); to frame an explanation that could replace the thing explained; and between numbers, dates or places meaning "sampai dengan" ("Tahun 2019—2022", "Senin—Jumat", "Jakarta—Bandung"). So never delete a dash by rule. What a machine overuses is the dash as an all-purpose joint. That is a style judgement, not an official rule.
- **Tanda hubung (-)**: reduplication (*anak-anak*), numeric dates (11-11-2022), scores (2-1), *se-Indonesia*, *ke-2*, *pasca-*, two elements as one unit (*suami-istri*).
- ***di*, *ke*, *dari* as prepositions are written apart** (*di mana*, *ke luar kota*); the prefix *di-* is attached (*dibaca*).
- ***Anda* is capitalised** ("Sudahkah Anda tahu?"). *Bapak*, *Ibu* and *Saudara* are capitalised when they address or replace a name ("Kapan Bapak berangkat?"), lowercase with a possessive ("bapak dan ibu kita"). *kamu* and *-mu* are lowercase.
- A comma follows a connective that opens a sentence.

## Numbers, dates, money

- The dot is the **thousands separator for counts**: *7.000.000 orang*. No dot in years or identity numbers: *tahun 1998*. Keep to numbers under a billion so the page does not read as an address.
- The **comma is the decimal mark**: *12,5 m*, *Rp750,00*. Money: *Rp5.000,00*, no space after *Rp*. A round amount may be part words: *90 juta rupiah*.
- A number that can be said in one word is written in letters in running text (*tiga kali*, *seribu buku*); measures, money and percentages take figures (*5%* or *7 persen*). Do not start a sentence with a number of several words: write it in letters or reorder.
- **Time:** *pukul 14.00* (dot). **Dates:** month and day names are capitalised: *23 November 2026*, *hari Jumat*. A letter's dateline has no full stop after the date: *Jakarta, 12 Oktober 2021*.
- In text that a reader will hear (voice, captions for speech), spell amounts out: *dua puluh lima juta rupiah*.

## Address (second person)

No official rule says when to use *Anda*, *kamu*, *Saudara* or *Bapak/Ibu*. EYD V only sets the capitals. *Bentuk dan Pilihan Kata* says the level of respect depends on the listener's age, status and closeness. Use `references/registers.md` for the choice, and keep one form through a whole text.

## Loanwords

Official equivalents exist: *unduh* (download), *unggah* (upload), *pindai* (scan). Real products mix them ("Download" on a button, "unduh" in the text around it). One product, one choice, used the same everywhere. Do not "fix" a loanword in a quote, a product name or a technical term.

## Edit only what you can back

When you correct something because "it is wrong", you should be able to name the rule above. When the fix is a matter of taste (a dash, a connective, a triad), say that it is style, or just do it quietly. Do not tell an author that something they wrote is ungrammatical if the only support is a blog.
