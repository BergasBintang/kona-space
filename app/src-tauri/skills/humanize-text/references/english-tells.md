# English tells: what machine-made text does, and what to do instead

Read the whole text once and mark what you find, strongest first. One strong tell is enough to act on. A weak tell alone is not: look for several together.

The patterns come from how language models write: they pick the likeliest next word, so they reach for the safe, general, important-sounding one. People who write well pick the specific one.

## A. Staging instead of stating (strong)

The text announces, contrasts or dramatizes instead of saying the thing.

| Tell | Example | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| "Not X but Y" contrast nobody asked for | "It's not just a tool, it's a movement." "This isn't about speed. It's about trust." | Say Y. Keep the contrast only if a real reader holds X. |
| A one-line closer after each section | "That is the real win." "And that changes everything." | Delete it, or move its content into the section. |
| A saying that sounds deep | "At its core, good design is about people." | Replace it with the specific fact it stands for, or cut it. |
| A run-up before the point | "Let's dive in." "Here's the thing." "Honestly? It depends." | Start at the point. |
| Arguing with a view nobody holds | "A tempting approach would be to start with the code." | State the approach you recommend and why. |
| A question the text then answers | "So what does this mean for you? Simple:" | Say the answer. |

## B. Inflation and borrowed authority (strong)

Importance is claimed, not shown.

- **Significance words:** "pivotal", "crucial", "vital", "testament to", "stands as", "marks a turning point", "underscores", "highlights the importance of", "plays a key role". Say what happened and let the reader judge.
- **Promotional words:** "vibrant", "rich", "breathtaking", "nestled", "boasts", "state-of-the-art", "seamless", "robust", "cutting-edge", "game-changer". Replace with a measurable detail or cut.
- **Vague experts:** "experts say", "studies show", "many believe", "industry reports suggest". Name who, or cut the claim. Never invent the name.
- **Trailing -ing phrases that add nothing:** "…, highlighting its importance and reflecting broader trends." Cut the tail or turn it into a sentence with a fact.
- **Hollow verbs for "is" and "has":** "serves as", "stands as", "features", "boasts", "offers". Use "is" and "has".
- **Challenges-and-future paragraph:** "Despite these challenges, it continues to thrive. The future looks bright." Replace with the actual open problem or a dated plan, or cut.
- **Hedging stack:** "could potentially perhaps be argued to". One hedge, only where the doubt is real.

## C. Rhythm by rule

- **Triads:** three items because three feels complete ("fast, simple, and reliable"). Keep the items that are true and different. Two is fine. One is fine.
- **Same sentence opening, three times in a row.** Vary the start, or join the sentences.
- **Same length everywhere.** Mix a short sentence with a longer one that carries a reason.
- **Every paragraph shaped as claim, three supports, recap.** Let a paragraph end when its point is made.
- **Connective on every sentence:** "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "In conclusion". Keep one only where the logic needs it.
- **Dashes as the all-purpose joint (weak alone).** If a dash does the work of a comma, colon or full stop, use that. Keep a dash the writer chose.

## D. Words that cluster in machine text (weak alone, strong in groups)

delve, tapestry, landscape (figurative), realm, navigate (a topic), foster, leverage, harness, unlock, elevate, streamline, multifaceted, nuanced, comprehensive, holistic, pivotal, intricate, meticulous, ever-evolving, in today's fast-paced world, it's important to note that, it's worth noting.

One of these in a text is nothing. Five in a page is the tell. Replace each with the plain word ("use", "look at", "help", "complex") or with the fact.

## E. Formatting by rule

- **Bold as decoration:** bold on terms the reader does not need to find. Bold a label only where people scan for it.
- **Bold lead-in on every bullet.** Turn the list into sentences if the points connect.
- **Title Case headings, emoji headings, headings that are slogans.** Use plain sentence-case headings that say what is below.
- **A heading repeated by the first sentence under it.** Start with the new fact.
- **Lists where prose would do,** and tables for two items.
- **Curly quotes mixed with straight ones (weak).** Follow the document.

## F. Leftovers from the chat

Delete without replacement: "Great question!", "Certainly!", "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if you'd like me to…", "As an AI…", "Here is the revised version:", knowledge-limit notes ("as of my last update"), apologies, offers of more help, text addressed to the person who asked rather than to the reader.

## G. Writing about the document

"This section explains…", "The table below compares…", "This function was added to…", "compiled from several sources". Say the thing itself. Keep a pointer only where the reader needs to find something.

## H. The wrong reader

A reply rebuilds the diagnosis, the proof and the background before it gives the decision, for someone who already has them. Lead with the decision or the answer, then the reasons the reader needs, then stop.

## I. Voice

Machine text has no one in it. Do not fix that by inventing a person. Add only what is true and known: a specific detail from the source, a real preference the writer stated, a mixed feeling the text already holds. If a detail is missing, ask for it or write the plainer sentence. Never add an anecdote, a quote, a number, or "in my experience".

## When not to act

- Inside a quotation, a title, a name, a legal or contract clause, a patent claim, a form, or a text that discusses these very phrases.
- Greetings and sign-offs that predate chatbots ("Let me know if you have questions" in an ordinary email).
- Text written before late 2022 was not written by today's models. Old text with these habits is just old style: edit it only if the owner asked for the style change.
- One weak tell alone.
- Specific details, honest mixed feelings, first-person choices, asides and era-bound references. They are what you are trying to keep.

## Do not promise detection results

No edit makes a text "undetectable", and these tells are habits, not proof of who wrote a text. Never say a text "passes" a detector. The goal is a text a reader enjoys and trusts.
