# Landing page words

One page, one reader, one action. Never invent numbers, customers or quotes. If there is no proof yet, leave a marked gap instead of making it up.

1. **Get the message first.** If there is none, use `positioning`.
2. **Know where they came from.** An ad, a search, a friend? What do they already know, and what do they doubt?
3. **Write the top first.** Someone who reads only this should know what it is, who it is for, and what to do next. Say what they get, not what it is called. No slogan that could fit any company.
4. **Then answer their doubts in order,** as far as needed:
   - What exactly do I get?
   - Does it work for someone like me? (true proof)
   - How does it work? (three steps at most)
   - What does it cost? What happens after I click?
   - What could go wrong? (questions and answers)
5. **One action,** repeated. The button says what happens: "Start free" beats "Submit". Say what lowers the risk beside it.
6. **Cut.** Remove "innovative", "seamless", "powerful" and "we are passionate". Replace each with a fact. Short words. A fact beats an adjective.
7. **Read it aloud.** Would a person say this to a friend?

Give the words section by section, with a note on each section's job. For a headline, offer three that are truly different, with the one you would pick first. List every claim that needs proof. The owner decides what is published.
