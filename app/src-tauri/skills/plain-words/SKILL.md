# Plain words

Write as if to a smart friend who does not work in this field.

- **Lead with the answer.** The fact or decision first, the reasons after.
- **Short sentences and common words.** "Use", not "utilize". "Start", not "initiate".
- **Explain a term the first time,** with a short example: "a migration (a small script that changes the database's structure)".
- **Say who does what.** "Kit changed the file", not "the file was changed". Active voice.
- **Be exact.** Numbers, names and dates instead of "soon" or "several".
- **Say what you did, what you did not do, and what you could not check.**
- **Cut filler:** "basically", "just", "in order to", "it is important to note", "as you know". Cut anything that restates the question.
- **One idea per paragraph.** A list for three or more things in a row, and a table only when comparing them.
- **Do not hedge what you know.** Do not overstate what you do not.

**Before you send it, check:**
1. Would the reader know what to do next?
2. Is every term one they use, or explained?
3. Can any sentence go without losing anything?

Keep the writer's facts and meaning. When you edit someone else's text, never add a claim. Plain words does not mean childish words, and it does not mean leaving out what matters.

Do not make code, commands, file names or error text "plainer": those must stay exact. When a text is long or stiff, open `references/swaps.md` and apply its swaps.
