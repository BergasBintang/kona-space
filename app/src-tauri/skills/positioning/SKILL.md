# Positioning

If it is unclear who the product is for, no amount of good writing will help. Never invent numbers, customers or quotes.

1. **Collect what is true.** What the product does today, not what it will do. What real users say, in their own words: reviews, support messages, what the owner has heard. Mark any guess as a guess.
2. **Name the best-fit person.** Not "small businesses". A person with a situation: "a one-person shop owner who still takes orders in chat". Who is it clearly not for?
3. **What do they use now?** Often a spreadsheet, a chat group or nothing, rather than a competitor. That is what you are compared with.
4. **What is different?** Only what is true and hard to copy. Then what it means to them. "Runs on your own PC" is a fact. "Your files never leave your computer" is why they care.
5. **Pick one main idea.** Saying three things means saying nothing.
6. **Test the sentence:** could a competitor say the same? Then it is not yet yours. Would a stranger understand it in one reading?

Open `templates/message.md` and fill it in. Give two or three versions with the one you would pick first, and say what you could not back up. Next: `landing-copy` or `launch-note`.
