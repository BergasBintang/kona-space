# Status update

Lead with what the reader has to know or do. A status update is not a diary.

1. **Collect the facts:** `list_tickets`, recent notes, and the commits if you can see them. Use only what you can point at. Do not guess at what is going on with a ticket.
2. **Write in this order:**
   - **In one sentence:** on track or not, and why.
   - **Needs you:** decisions or answers only the owner can give. This goes first because it is the only part they must act on.
   - **Done:** finished work, in terms of what it gives, not ticket numbers.
   - **Under way:** who is on what.
   - **Stuck:** what is blocking it, since when, and what would free it.
   - **Next:** what comes after this.
3. **Short.** A busy person should finish it in under a minute. Five lines is often enough; leave out empty headings.
4. **Be honest.** Bad news gets said plainly, near the top. Something that has been "almost done" for a week is stuck: say so.
5. **Say how old the facts are** and what you could not see.

If the reader is outside the team, take out names and inside jargon, and say what changes for them.
