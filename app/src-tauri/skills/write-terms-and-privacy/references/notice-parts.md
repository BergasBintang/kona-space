# What a privacy notice must tell people, and how to ask for consent

From Law 27 of 2022 (UU PDP). Article text was read on a public site that lists the law by article, not on the official gazette: a lawyer checks the numbers. Pasal means article.

## When the basis is consent, the person is told (Pasal 21(1))

Seven things, each with its own place in the notice:

1. The legal basis of the processing.
2. The purpose.
3. The kind and the relevance of the data.
4. How long the documents holding the data are kept (retention).
5. The details of the information collected: what exactly, not "your information".
6. How long the processing lasts.
7. The person's rights.

If any of this changes, tell the person before the change takes effect (Pasal 21(2)). So the notice needs a "when we change this" part that says how people are told (an e-mail, a message in the product) and what they must do.

## The six legal bases (Pasal 20(2))

Say which one you rely on, for each purpose. A purpose can have a different basis from another.
- Explicit valid consent for one or more stated purposes.
- Performing a contract with the person, or steps they asked for before one (a sign-up, an order).
- A legal duty of the company (tax records).
- Protecting a person's vital interests.
- Public interest, public service or public authority.
- Other legitimate interests, weighed against the person's rights.

Do not write "consent" for everything. Using the service you offer is usually the contract. A newsletter or analytics is usually consent.

## How consent is asked (Pasal 22 to 24)

- It is written or recorded, on paper or electronic. Both count equally (22(1) to (3)).
- When the same request also covers other matters, the consent request must be clearly separate from them, easy to understand and find, and in simple clear language. If not, it is void by the law (22(4), (5)). So: never one tick for "terms and privacy and marketing". One choice per purpose, unticked, with its own sentence.
- A contract clause that takes data without valid explicit consent is void (23). Do not write one.
- The company must be able to show proof that the person agreed (24). The notice does not need to say how, but the product must record who, when and which version of the text. If it does not, raise a ticket.
- Children's data and the data of people with disabilities need consent from a parent or guardian, and special handling (25, 26). If the product is used by children, the notice says how a parent agrees, in words a parent understands.

## Rights, and what to say about them (Pasal 5 to 15)

Tell people, in plain words, that they can ask for: information (5), correction (6), access and a copy (7), deletion or destruction (8), to withdraw consent (9), to object to a decision made only by a machine that matters to them (10), to pause or limit processing (11), and their data in a form another service can read (13). Requests go in as a recorded request, by e-mail or on paper (14): give one real channel and a name or team for it. Say what happens next: the law sets 3 x 24 hours for correcting, for access, for stopping after consent is withdrawn, and for restricting; and the person is told the result (30, 32, 40, 41). Only promise what the product and team can really do. If a deadline cannot be met, say what you can meet, or raise a ticket first. A few rights stop applying in special cases such as national defence, law enforcement and statistics (15, 50): normal products do not need to mention them.

## Other things the notice should say

- **Who we are:** the company that decides the purposes (the controller), its address and a contact for data questions. If a data protection officer is appointed (53), name the role and the contact. `[TBD]` if not known.
- **Who else gets the data:** the kinds of other parties (payment provider, e-mail sender, analytics, hosting) and why. Name them if the owner agrees. A processor works only on the company's orders (51).
- **Abroad:** if data goes to a service outside Indonesia, say which country or region and what protects it (55, 56). The law asks for equal or better protection, or binding safeguards, or the person's consent.
- **Security:** say what is really done, in general terms ("passwords are stored in a form we cannot read"), never a promise of "100% safe".
- **Keeping and deleting:** for each kind of data, how long and what happens at the end: delete or destroy (42 to 45), and that the person is told when it is done (45).
- **If something goes wrong:** the company will tell the person and the agency in writing within 3 x 24 hours of a data failure, with what was exposed, when and how, and what is being done (46). Include this only if the company can really do it.
- **Cookies and trackers:** list the kinds in use, what each is for and how to refuse the ones that need consent.
- **Changes:** how people are told before something changes (21(2)).
- **Language and date:** the date of the version and "last changed". Keep old versions so consent records can point to them.

## Words to avoid

"Your privacy is important to us", "we may share with trusted partners", "for any other purpose we think fit" and "to improve your experience" tell the person nothing and do not meet 21(1). Replace each with the thing: which partner kind, which purpose, what data.
