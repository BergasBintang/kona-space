# The parts of Terms and Conditions, and the traps in each

Terms are the rules between the company and the person who uses the product. They must be true to what the product does, short enough to read, and kept apart from the privacy notice and from consent for data. Anything marked `(to verify)` is general knowledge, not read for this skill: a lawyer checks it.

## Parts, in the order a person needs them

1. **Who and what.** The company name, its form, address and contact. What the product is, in two sentences. `[TBD]` for anything unknown.
2. **Who may use it.** Minimum age; whether a parent must agree; whether it is for businesses only. If children use it, say so and link to the privacy notice's part on children.
3. **The account.** What the person must keep true (their details), keep safe (their password), and tell you (a lost account). What you do if you see misuse.
4. **What you may do and not do.** A short list of real rules (no attacking the service, no illegal content, no misuse of other people's data). Only rules the company will enforce.
5. **What the person puts in.** Who owns their content, and the licence the company needs to run the service (store it, show it to the people the person chose). Keep the licence as narrow as the product needs.
6. **What the company owns.** The product, name and design stay the company's. The person gets the right to use it, not to copy it.
7. **Money.** Only if there is any: prices, taxes, when and how charged, renewals, how to cancel, refunds and the days they take. Every number must match the checkout. If you do not know, `[TBD]`.
8. **Changes and the service itself.** How you tell people about changes to the product or the terms, and what they can do if they disagree (stop using it, close the account).
9. **Ending.** How the person leaves, when the company may suspend or close an account, what happens to the data afterwards (point to the privacy notice) and what stays valid after ending.
10. **When things go wrong.** What the company is and is not responsible for. See the trap below.
11. **Law and disputes.** Which law and which place settle disputes. For users in Indonesia: Indonesian law is the usual choice, and a local place for disputes `(to verify)`. Do not write a foreign court for consumers without a lawyer.
12. **Contact and the rest.** Where to write, the date of this version, and a line that the privacy notice is a separate document and is part of how the person is told about their data.

## Traps

- **One page for everything.** Terms, privacy and consent are three different things. The terms may link to the privacy notice; they do not contain the consent. Consent for marketing or analytics is asked separately, with its own unticked choice (UU PDP Pasal 22(4)).
- **Taking away data rights.** A clause where the person gives up access, deletion, correction or withdrawal of consent is void under the personal data law (Pasal 22 and 23). Do not write one.
- **"We are not responsible for anything."** A blanket denial of all responsibility toward consumers is likely not allowed under Indonesian consumer law on standard clauses (UU 8/1999, Pasal 18) `(to verify)`. Write the specific limits that are true: no promise of uninterrupted service, no responsibility for what other people post, a cap on what you pay `(to verify with a lawyer)`.
- **Promises the product does not keep.** "Backed up daily", "available 24/7", "refund in 7 days": keep only what the team does today.
- **Hidden changes.** Do not say terms can change at any time with no notice. Say how and when you tell people.
- **Wording that sounds like a statute.** Short sentences. The test: could the owner read a clause aloud to a customer without explaining it?
- **Copying.** Do not paste another company's terms. Different facts, different promises, and a copyright problem.
- **Two languages that disagree.** If there are Indonesian and English versions, say which one wins, and keep them the same.
- **E-signatures and electronic contracts.** The rules on electronic agreements and "click to accept" are in other laws (the electronic information law and its regulations) `(to verify)`. Make acceptance an action the person takes, record it with date and version, and keep the old versions.

## Industry parts to add when the facts say so

Online shop (delivery, returns, damaged goods), subscription (renewal, cancel, price changes), marketplace (the company is the middleman, seller duties, disputes between people), health or finance (strong rules from other laws `(to verify)`), school or child product (parents, safety, content rules), tool for businesses (the business is the controller of its customers' data and the product is the processor: a data processing agreement is needed, which this skill does not write).
