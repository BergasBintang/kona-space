# Kona art kit

Pixel art for the office (ticket T-0001, decisions 44 and 45 in `kona-space-app.md`). A first pass drawn by script, to be refined by the owner in LibreSprite.

- `node build-cats.mjs` writes `out/cats.png` (a sprite sheet: a row per role and look, a column per frame; the cat has four looks, one block of rows each), `out/cats.json` (the frame size, order, roles and number of looks) and a 5x preview.
- `node build-office.mjs` draws the building from all four sides and writes `out/office-with-cats.png` and `-v1`, `-v2`, `-v3` (the whole building with its cats, to look at), `out/parts/` (a small picture for every piece) and `out/views/v0.json` to `v3.json` (see below). It stops if the parts of a side do not make its picture pixel for pixel, and prints a CHECK line for every rule a piece breaks. `node to-app.mjs` then copies the views, the pieces they name and the cat sheet into `app/src/office/` and removes what no side names any more.
- `lib.mjs` is a small pixel canvas (no packages). `cat.mjs` is the cat and the role icons. `office.mjs` is the building.
- The floor plan is the list `DEFS` at the top of `office.mjs`: a rectangle of tiles per room, so a room can be moved or resized by editing one line. The plan is a real office: a hall, rooms of any size, solid walls and one door per room onto the hall (the script refuses a room that has no border with the hall, or two rooms that overlap). The left edge and the back edge must stay straight (they carry the brick walls). Add a room only when a screen of the app needs one. A bigger picture is shown smaller by the app (whole-number scale), so check the size line after a build. - The building is made of parts: a small picture for every piece (a piece of floor, a wall, a post, a desk, a chair, a plant, a board, a rug, the brick back walls; pieces that look the same share a file), listed in `out/views/vN.json` with its place, its front-to-back number `d`, the room it is the floor of, and `hides`: the rooms it stands in front of. The cats are in the same list so the app draws a cat between the pieces behind it and in front of it. The app draws the list on one canvas; when the owner goes into a room it leaves out the walls that `hides` that room.
- The building is drawn from all four sides (`setView(0..3)` in `office.mjs`; each view is one quarter turn of the camera). `out/views/v0.json` to `v3.json` hold, for each side, the list of pieces, the rooms with their outlines and name plates, and the cats. The app reads all four and turns the building with two buttons. Furniture is built on whole tiles (`piece()` and `frame()` in `office.mjs`: a footprint of whole tiles, boxes of whole 4 px steps, a front that faces one of four ways, small details painted on a face or a top). The build checks all four views and prints no CHECK line today. The rules that keep it so: a low piece (under 16 px) keeps floor of its own room on all four sides, because from some side a wall stands in front of it; a cat's chair is a low piece, and a tall piece stands two tiles from a cat. The cats have four looks (`cat(role, frame, dir)` in `cat.mjs`: 0 looks down-left, a three-quarter view with the face and belly turned left, shaded on the right, with a tail; 2 looks up-left, seen from behind. 1 and 3 are 0 and 2 mirrored); Kibby, the office boy, has his own sheet (`kibby.mjs`, `out/kibby.png`: a row per look, a column per frame, including four walking frames) and a route on the hall (`WALK` in `office.mjs`), written into every view's file under `walk` with his place in the drawing order for every eighth of a tile; the build checks every step like it checks the cats. `out/cats.png` holds a block of rows for each look, and the parts list says which look each cat has. Windows and wall art go on the inner face of every outer wall and show only on the tall back walls of the current view.
- The walls are made by `walls.mjs`, not by hand. `office.mjs` only says where walls stand (on every border between rooms except doors, on the outer side of every room, and tall brick on every side of the building that is behind the camera) and `walls.mjs` draws all of them as one structure and cuts it into pieces, so a single wall, the end or middle of a run, a corner, a T and a door post all come out right in every view.
- The pieces, the cat sheet and the four views are the only files the app reads; `to-app.mjs` keeps them in step. `out/parts/` and `out/views/` are made again by every build and are not in git (the app keeps its own copy).
- Everything is placed on a grid of 1/8 tile, so slanted edges have clean, even steps. Do not use other fractions for a piece or a detail.
- `node build-frames.mjs` writes the tiled frames (paper, teal and red, large 18 x 18 and small 12 x 12, each in normal, hover and pressed looks) and a 4x preview of each. They are for buttons, cards and dialogs: the corners stay, the edges repeat, the middle is empty. In CSS: `border: 12px solid transparent; background-clip: padding-box; border-image: url(frame-wood-normal.png) 6 / 12px repeat; image-rendering: pixelated;`. Tested in Edge at several sizes; not yet used in the app.
- Walls take space: a wall is a slab a quarter of a tile thick centred on the tile edge, so a box that starts on a tile edge stands half inside it. `frame().b` in `office.mjs` cuts every box back to the face of any wall slab it overlaps (`clipToWalls`, `SLABS`), so furniture against a wall is never sunk into it; the build lists the pieces it cut and prints a CHECK line for a box that lies across a wall. The one door onto the outside is `EXIT` (the Entrance, east wall): an opening with two posts where the wall is low, a door painted on the brick where it is tall. Keep two tiles inside it clear.
- The floor slab (the tan base under the building) follows the outer face of the walls, not the tile edge: `office.mjs` builds the ground from the tiles plus the wall slabs (corners filled) and draws its front edges, so a wall never hangs over its base. Where a room with an outer wall meets the open edge of the hall the base steps by the wall's thickness (1/8 tile); that is the wall, not a fault. There are no plants (removed 2026-10-31, purely decorative).
- The floor is made of one tile picture per colour (`tiles.mjs`, 33 x 17 px): the build copies it onto every tile of a room, so there is no per-tile drawing rule and no grout line is cut by the next tile. `node build-tiles.mjs` writes the 32 tiles into `art/tiles/<room id>-<0 or 1>.png` (it never overwrites one); a file there is used instead of the one the script makes, so a tile can be drawn by hand in LibreSprite. Keep the size and the top corner at x = 16, and keep the rim pixels the same colour in a tile with grout.
- Turned sides: a floor tile's top corner is the highest of its four corners, not its (i, j) corner (`office.mjs`, "the floor"). The open edge of the hall has no wall, but the building's rim is as wide as at a room (the base runs straight along the whole side), so the strip a wall would stand on is painted as hall floor.
